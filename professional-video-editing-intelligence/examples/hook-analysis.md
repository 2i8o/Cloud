# Worked Example A - A 10-Second Curiosity Hook

**Brief (hypothetical):** "A 10-second opening for a short video about the Moon looking bigger near the horizon. General audience. Must lead into a ~35-second explanation."

This example shows the framework applied end to end, including a failed first draft. It is a **planning exercise**; no video was rendered. Facts to verify before production are flagged.

## Step 1 - Contract (hook <-> payoff)

Facts I rely on (confidence): the Moon's *angular size* is essentially the same at the horizon and overhead - **Established**, and checkable with a photo or a measuring method (e.g., the same object held at arm's length covers the same fraction of the Moon in both positions). The Moon looks larger near the horizon - an **illusion**, **Established**. *Why* the illusion occurs - **no single accepted explanation; several proposals are debated** (**Guess** on my side to rank them; verify with a current review article before scripting an explanation).

So the honest promise cannot be "the secret reason." The promise is: *"It looks bigger, it isn't, and here is how to prove it yourself."* The unresolved cause becomes honest content, not a withheld secret.

**Question:** "Is the Moon really bigger near the horizon?"  **Answer:** "No - you can measure that; the effect is in perception, and why exactly is still debated."

## Draft v1 (fails)

| Time | Visual | Audio | Text |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-2 | Logo sting | Whoosh + music swell | Channel name |
| 2-5 | Big orange Moon over skyline | VO: "The Moon is HUGE near the horizon!" | "HUGE MOON" |
| 5-8 | Fast zoom on Moon with flash cuts | Risers | "Why??" |
| 8-10 | Title card "The Moon Illusion" | Hit | - |

**Diagnosis against the framework**
- Integrity (I): "The Moon is HUGE" is false as a physical claim (fails the integrity gate; level 1 of the ladder).
- Hook structure: logo first (delays the question); statement of topic, not a question; three channels fighting.
- Rhythm: flash cuts every 0.4 s before any information; no hold; photosensitivity risk from rapid flashes.
- Sound: whoosh + riser + hit on a 10 s clip = unmotivated.
- Visual question: absent - the image does not set up a *test*.

## Alternatives considered

| Option | Strength | Weakness | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Misconception flip: "You've been told the Moon is bigger at the horizon. Measure it." | Honest, sets up proof | Risks sounding preachy | **Chosen** |
| B. Paradox image: same photo twice (horizon / high), identical sizes overlaid | Instantly visual | Needs two real photos/shots at matching focal length | **Chosen as the visual** |
| C. "Scientists can't agree why" | Truthful curiosity | Might feel vague as a first-second hook | Use as beat 3 |
| D. Personal-stake test ("I measured it") | Strong | Needs real measurement footage | Optional upgrade |

## Draft v2

| Time | Visual | Sound | Text | Job |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0-1.0 | Frame 0: low, huge-looking Moon over a horizon line (real photo/footage, long lens) | Quiet night ambience only | none | Image creates the felt premise |
| 1.0-3.0 | Slow push-in. Voice: "Looks huge, right?" | VO, ambience under | "Is it really bigger?" (one line, large) | State the question in two channels |
| 3.0-5.5 | Cut (direct) to the *same Moon high in the sky*, same framing scale | Ambience dips for 6-8 frames | - | Contrast sets up the paradox |
| 5.5-8.0 | Overlay: a circle measured on the horizon Moon snaps to the high Moon - they match | One soft tick on the match (1 sound) | "Same size." | Evidence arrives: the premise is wrong |
| 8.0-10.0 | Hold on the matched circles; VO: "So why does your brain say otherwise?" | Near-silence, then direct cut into content | question text stays | Hand-off: new, honest question |

Cut points sit at thought boundaries (after "right?", after "Same size.", before the new question). Total: 5 beats, 10 s, one sound accent, no logo.

## Review against the rubric

| Dimension | Score | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| A Clarity | 3 | Question stated at 1-3 s, answered at 5.5 s, new question at 8 s |
| B Emotion | 2 | Wonder -> surprise -> renewed curiosity; hold at 5.5-8 gives time |
| C Rhythm | 2 | Beat lengths 1.0 / 2.0 / 2.5 / 2.5 / 2.0 s - fairly even; improve by shortening beat 3 (3.0-4.5) and holding 5.5-8 longer |
| D Continuity | 3 | Same framing scale and lens look; direction consistent |
| E Sound | 3 | One sync accent; silence before hand-off |
| F Hierarchy | 2 | Single text line at a time; verify safe area |
| G Technical | 2 | Needs matching exposure/color of the two Moon shots - verify |
| H Restraint | 3 | No logo, flashes, or risers |
| I Integrity | 3 | No false claim; unresolved cause framed honestly |

Revision made after scoring: beat 3 shortened by 0.5 s and the match overlay extended by 0.5 s (payoff hold > build).

## Weaknesses that remain (honest)

- Requires two real, comparably framed shots; if they cannot be sourced, the paradox image must be constructed (a **composite must be labeled or avoided**, otherwise the "evidence" is staged).
- Measuring overlay must be to scale; use image measurement, not eyeballing.
- If the client wants a bigger first-second punch, test option D (personal measurement) as an A/B variant - change one thing only.

## What this test taught the skill

- The first draft failed because of a **false-premise hook**. The skill now includes the "misconception flip" pattern and its conditions in `references/hook-and-retention.md` (section 3), and the integrity gate's "unknown/debated" clause was exercised and kept.
