# Curiosity and attention - what the evidence supports (and what marketing adds)

Source IDs (S##) refer to `SOURCE_BIBLIOGRAPHY.md` / `RESEARCH_LEDGER.csv`. Almost all lab findings come from text, trivia, static images or long-form media - **none was tested on 20-45 s vertical video feeds**. Treat every "use" line as a hypothesis to test with the creator's own data.

Iraqi Arabic examples are **model-written drafts** for native review by the creator. Facts inside examples are illustrative unless a source ID is shown; verify before publishing.

Evidence tags: **E** established (replicated / meta-analytic / official), **L** likely (strong inference, partial transfer), **G** guess (practitioner heuristic or extrapolation).

---
## 1. Information-gap theory of curiosity [L]
- **Supports:** Curiosity is the felt deprivation of noticing a specific gap between what you know and what you want to know (Loewenstein 1994, S11; formalised as question-answer gaps by Golman & Loewenstein 2018, S15). In fMRI, curiosity about trivia answers tracked reward-anticipation circuitry; people paid tokens/time for answers when more curious (Kang 2009, S12).
- **Overstated:** "Open a gap and viewers must watch." The theory needs a *specific, recognised* question. Vague teasers do not create gaps.
- **Hook use:** Name the exact unknown, not a mood. One gap per hook.
- **Fails when:** the gap is trivial, already known, or the payoff is cheaper than the wait.
- **Good:** `20/20 مو يعني نظرك مثالي. يعني نظرك… عادي.` (S44) **Bad:** `شنو سر النظر المثالي؟`

## 2. Knowledge gaps and partial knowledge [L]
- **Supports:** Curiosity was highest at intermediate confidence and lower when people had no idea or were sure (Kang 2009; summarised in Kidd & Hayden 2015, S14). Infants look away from both too-predictable and too-surprising events (S14).
- **Overstated:** a universal inverted-U for every audience. Evidence is trivia + infants.
- **Use:** anchor the hook in something half-known (20/20, glasses, blue light).
- **Fails when:** topic is cold (viewer knows nothing) or fully known.
- **Good:** `كلنا سمعنا 20/20… بس شنو يعني هذول الرقمين؟` **Bad:** `ما هي المسافة المرجعية في كسر سنلن؟`

## 3. Prediction error and expectation violation [L]
- **Supports:** Surprise defined as change in beliefs (Bayesian surprise) predicted where people looked in natural video: 72% of gaze shifts landed on above-average-surprise locations (Itti & Baldi 2009, S26).
- **Overstated:** "Pattern-break = retention." That paper concerns gaze in free viewing, not scroll decisions or watch-through.
- **Use:** violate a belief the viewer actually holds (a myth), then resolve it.
- **Fails when:** the violated expectation is not the viewer's, or the resolution is false.
- **Good:** `يگولون النظارة تضعّف العين. أكاديمية طب العيون الأمريكية تگول لا.` (AAO myth 14, S35) **Bad:** `ما راح تصدق شنو يصير للعين!`

## 4. Surprise vs meaningful surprise [L]
- **Supports:** Surprise only matters when it changes beliefs (S26); curiosity enhanced memory especially for answers people had guessed wrongly (S12).
- **Overstated:** random shock. A shocking image unrelated to the idea creates a gap about the image, not the topic.
- **Use:** ensure the surprise IS the insight.
- **Good:** `ثقب صغير بورقة… والسطر المشوّش يصير واضح.` (pinhole, S45, S46) **Bad:** `شوف هذا الحيوان الغريب!` then lens talk.

## 5. Novelty, relevance and attention [L/G]
- **Supports:** novelty alone is weak; relevance to the viewer's own situation and prior knowledge increases engagement with narratives (Green 2004: prior knowledge -> more transportation, S30).
- **Overstated:** "novelty first". Creators (Galloway S52) pair novelty with core/casual/new-viewer fit - a practitioner heuristic [G].
- **Use:** novel mechanism + familiar situation (burning eyes after reels).
- **Good:** `عيونك تحترق بعد ساعة ريلز؟ تغمّض أقل من المعتاد.` (AAO blink-rate, S32) **Bad:** `أغرب شي بالكون: العين!`

## 6. Cognitive load and processing fluency [E/L]
- **Supports:** Easy-to-process statements are judged truer/more likeable (fluency review, Alter & Oppenheimer 2009, S22). Multimedia design that removes extraneous material and adds signalling improves learning (coherence principle supported in 10 of 11 tests, median d 1.32; Mayer 2005, S23); a 2025 meta-analysis of Mayer's corpus gives overall g = 0.37 with principle-level variation (S24).
- **Overstated:** "simpler is always better" - fluency is a *cue*, and people discount it when the source is obviously irrelevant (S22). Fluency can make false claims feel true -> a reason for stricter fact checks, not looser.
- **Use:** one idea per sentence; hide nothing the viewer needs to parse the first image.
- **Good:** `هاي الجملة مشوّشة. هسه شوف.` **Bad:** `العدسة التصحيحية ذات القوة الكروية السالبة تؤخر تقارب الأشعة…`

## 7. Specificity and concrete language [L]
- **Supports:** Across 8,977 news-headline experiments, concreteness helps CTR when headlines in context are too vague and hurts when they are already too concrete: a curvilinear relation (Le Quéré & Matias 2025, S18).
- **Overstated:** "be specific" or "be mysterious" as absolutes.
- **Use:** specific *question*, withheld *answer*. Specific facts only if verified.
- **Good:** `حسب AAO، حوالي 35% بس من البالغين (بأمريكا) يشوفون 20/20 بدون نظارة.` (S44; one clinician quoted, US data -> label L) **Bad:** `فحص النظر مهم لكل الناس.`

## 8. Perceived relevance and personal stakes [L]
- **Supports:** transported readers adopt story-consistent beliefs (S17); relevance/similarity to the recipient raises transportation (S30).
- **Overstated:** "make it personal with fear" - see 16.
- **Use:** recognisable situation, not a threat. **Good:** `طفلك يگعد گريب من التلفزيون؟ ما يخرّب عيونه، بس ممكن يحتاج نظارة.` (AAO myth 10, S35) **Bad:** `كل الأطفال بخطر!`

## 9. Uncertainty, ambiguity and comprehension [L]
- **Supports:** the gap must be *understood* to be felt; ambiguity the viewer cannot parse reads as noise (S18 vague-headline branch).
- **Rule:** the viewer must grasp the question in <= 3 s without sound (see hook-evaluation).
- **Good:** `شنو غلط بهذي السطور؟ (٣ ثواني) هسه شوف.` **Bad:** `شي غريب يصير بعيونك…`

## 10. Questions and resolution [L]
- **Supports:** forward-reference headlines capture attention quickly (ERP study, Li et al. 2022, S31) and raise curiosity (Pengnate 2021, S19), but reduce perceived knowledge quality (S19) and credibility (Kaushal & Vemuri 2021, S20). A Stanford pre-registered pair of experiments found *no* effect of clickbait on trust or learning (S21; working paper). Mixed evidence overall.
- **Rule:** answerable question, answered. Rhetorical generics ("هل تعلم؟") are overused and add nothing.
- **Good:** `ليش تختفي النقطة إذا غمّضت عين؟` (blind-spot demo; verify before use) **Bad:** `هل تعلم أن العين مهمة؟`

## 11. Expectation formation and fulfilment [L]
- **Supports:** YouTube's retention help ties a strong intro to matching the viewer's expectation set by packaging (S03); Lawrence lists "not meeting expectations set up in the packaging" as the first retention drop (S50) [practitioner]; Galloway/Colin & Samir stress the same (S52, S57) [practitioner].
- **Use:** every hook is a contract; write the payoff line first.
- **Fails when:** the promise is bigger than the evidence. **Bad:** `الحقيقة الي ما يگولها لك دكتور العيون`.

## 12. Narrative transportation [E for text/film; L for 30 s]
- **Supports:** absorption (attention + imagery + emotion) increases story-consistent beliefs, regardless of fact/fiction labelling (Green & Brock 2000, S17). Reduced counter-arguing is a known side-effect -> **ethical duty**: never use transport to carry a false claim.
- **Use:** micro-story with a real or clearly labelled hypothetical protagonist and a causal chain ("but/therefore", S56).
- **Rule:** no invented patient stories; if composite, say so on screen.

## 13. Suspense, anticipation and information sequencing [L]
- **Supports:** Spoilers did not reduce enjoyment in three story experiments (Leavitt & Christenfeld 2011, S27; a later study found spoilers did matter for TV episodes, Daniel & Katz 2019 - listed in PubMed, abstract not read here). Lawrence argues giving away the most valuable content early removes the reason to stay (S51) [practitioner].
- **Reconciliation [G]:** stating the *what* may not hurt if the *how/why/demo* is the payoff. Test on your own channel.
- **Good:** `النظارة ما تضعّف العين. بس ليش نعتقد العكس؟` **Bad:** hook that states the full mechanism and conclusion in 3 s.

## 14. The Zeigarnik effect - limited use [E that the memory claim is NOT reliable]
- **Supports:** A 2025 meta-analysis (38 publications) found no memory advantage for interrupted tasks (weighted ratio 0.99; dz = 0.15) but a reliable tendency to resume interrupted tasks (~67% resume; the Ovsiankina effect) (Ghibellini & Meier 2025, S16).
- **Rule:** never cite Zeigarnik as the reason a cliffhanger works. A cliffhanger without payoff is a broken promise (S19-S20), not a psychological law.

## 15. Pattern interruption [G; L for gaze]
- **Supports:** surprising visual events attract gaze (S26). No verified evidence in this research that interruption raises *watch-through* in feeds.
- **Use:** make the interruption informative (e.g. text suddenly clear). **Bad:** random zoom + "ستوب!".

## 16. Emotional salience without fear-mongering [E on effects; ethics is our rule]
- **Supports:** meta-analyses find fear appeals can change attitudes and behaviour on average (Witte & Allen 2000, S28; Tannenbaum 2015, d = 0.29, S29), more so with efficacy messages. Strong fear with low efficacy produces defensive avoidance (S28).
- **Our rule is ethical and factual, not "fear doesn't work":** an exaggerated threat is a fabricated claim. Use awe, relief, clarity, usefulness.
- **Good:** `ريلز ما يخرّب عيونك… بس تعبها له سبب.` **Bad:** `الشاشة راح تعميك.`

## 17. Visual attention and audiovisual congruence [E/L]
- **Supports:** captions improve comprehension, attention and memory across >100 studies (Gernsbacher 2015, S25); Mayer's redundancy principle (graphics + narration beat graphics + narration + on-screen text; supported in 10 of 10 tests, median d 0.69, S23) has boundary conditions (short text near graphics is fine). **Synthesis [L]:** put a 2-5 word key phrase on screen, not a transcript.
- **Rule:** the image must show the question; sound reinforces, never replaces (see hook-visual-design).
