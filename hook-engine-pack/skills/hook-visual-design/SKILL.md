# Visual and audio integration

The creator already produces high-quality visuals. This skill does not ask for more stimulation; it asks whether each visual/audio choice makes the **question** or the **payoff** clearer.

## Production brief (one per finalist)
1. **First frame** - what is readable in frame 1 (<= 3 elements to attend to; Galloway's 3-element rule is for thumbnails, applied here to the first frame as an *inference* [Guess], S52).
2. **First visible action** - the first meaningful visual event and when (a hypothesis: within ~1-2 s).
3. **Spoken opening** - the hook; sync the keyword to the visual reveal (word lands as the object appears).
4. **On-screen text** - 2-5 words max, the *key phrase*, not a transcript (Mayer redundancy + captions evidence, S23, S25).
5. **Sound cue** - semantic only (e.g. click when the card locks into place). Sound must reinforce, never carry the idea.
6. **Transition into beat 2** - what the eye/ear is asked to follow next; keep continuity of the object.
7. **Expected viewer question** - one line; if you cannot write it, the opening does not create a question.

## Checks
- **Sound-off test:** can a muted viewer state the question from frame/text alone? (Captions improve comprehension and attention across >100 studies, S25 - E; the share of feed viewing done without sound is **unverified** here: do not quote industry percentages.)
- **Glance test:** show frame 1 for ~1 s, then ask what is going on (Galloway's thumbnail test, S52 - practitioner, adapt as heuristic).
- **Decorate-or-clarify test:** remove the effect; if the idea is equally clear and equally interesting, the effect was decoration -> cut it. If the idea collapses without it, the effect is carrying the premise -> fix the premise (hook-evaluation).
- **Information density vs cut rhythm:** a cut should coincide with new information, not fill silence. Excess stimulation raises extraneous load (Mayer coherence principle, S23, S24).
- **Visual hierarchy:** one focal object; contrast and motion direct attention to it; no competing logos/text.
- **Text timing:** appears with the word it supports, stays long enough to read at one pass (rule of thumb: ~3 words per second, test on a colleague).

## Optics-specific accuracy rules (hard)
1. **Camera != eye.** A phone camera has different optics, exposure and sensor behaviour. Never present camera footage as "what your eye sees" unless the effect is verified for human vision; label simulations ("محاكاة").
2. **Screen demos depend on the viewer's screen/distance/brightness.** Acuity or colour demos on a phone are not tests (ISO 8596 itself is not a clinical measurement standard, S42; test conditions matter, S43). Say "ليس فحصاً".
3. **Before/after honesty:** same lighting, same distance, same exposure, no retouching of the "after". If any parameter differs, disclose it.
4. **No unsafe viewer actions** (sun gazing, lasers, strong lights close to the eye, rubbing eyes).
5. **Do not show a diagnosis** from a photo of an eye.
6. Accessibility: colour-vision-safe palettes for any red/green coding; captions on.
